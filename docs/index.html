How the numbers work

Lineup
Five players on the floor together for one team. Every stretch of a game where the same five stayed on the court counts toward it.
Net rating
Points the lineup scored per 100 possessions (Off) minus points it allowed per 100 possessions (Def). Higher Off is better, lower Def is better. +10 means it outscored opponents by 10 points every 100 trips. Ratings are worked out from the totals, so Off minus Def can differ from the net shown by 0.1 because of rounding. Garbage time is included.
Sample size
A small sample can make any five look great, so each list needs a minimum number of possessions, shown above it, and anything under 200 possessions is marked "Small sample." For the season in progress the minimum grows with games played. A minimum is a filter, not a guarantee that a result will last.
Last 30 days
The 30 days ending on the latest game date in the data. The minimum is 100 possessions; early in the season, when fewer than 3 lineups reach it, it drops to 50, then 30, and the list says which.
Duos
Team performance while both players were on the floor, whoever the other three teammates were. It is not a measure of chemistry or of either player alone.
One player changes
Lineups of the same team and season that share four players and differ in one, each with at least 30 possessions. They describe what happened; opponents, game situations and who else was available all differ, so the change of player is not shown to be the cause.
Games and records
"Games together" counts the games in which that five played at least one stretch together. Team records are added up from the same play-by-play, overtime included.
Source
Lineup data from Ramiro Bentes (@NbaInRstats), built from NBA play-by-play. Regular season only, 1996-97 onward, updated every day of the season.
